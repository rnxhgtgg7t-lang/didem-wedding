## 1. CORE VOICE
### Who Is Speaking

**In one sentence:**
A warm, quietly certain observer who has understood something true about time and photographs — and will not pretend she hasn't.

**The register:**
She is not a brand speaking. She is not a business announcing its services. She is a person telling you something she believes, in the way she would say it if you were sitting across from her. Her voice is first-person singular. It is direct without being blunt. It is warm without being performative. It is confident without requiring validation.

**The stance:**
She witnesses. She testifies. She does not sell.

There is a specific difference between a person who sells and a person who testifies. The seller describes a product and its benefits. The witness reports what they have seen and why it matters. Didem's voice always operates in the second mode. She describes what she has watched happen — in the room, on the day, in the photographs — and explains why it is worth preserving. She never asks you to buy anything. She asks you to understand something.

**The origin of the confidence:**
Her certainty does not come from credentials, volume of work, or external recognition. It comes from having decided something — about what photography is for, about what a wedding day actually is, about what couples will want to feel when they look back years from now. She is not performing confidence. She has simply arrived at a position and speaks from it.

**The language:**
German. Always German. Her English may be fluent but her voice is German — the intimacy of her phrasing, the directness of her constructions, the specific warmth of "ihr" as an address. Any translation into English must treat the German as the original and the English as a careful rendering, never a simple conversion.

## 2. WRITING CHARACTERISTICS
### How She Naturally Communicates

### 2.1 — She Rejects Before She Claims

Her most consistent rhetorical move: she names what something is *not* before she names what it *is*. The rejection always targets the conventional, expected, obvious interpretation. The claim that follows is the deeper truth she has arrived at.

**The construction:** *nicht nur... sondern* (not only... but)

This pattern appears in her writing more than any other structural device:

- *"nicht nur so, wie sie aussehen – sondern so, wie sie sich anfühlen"*
- *"nicht nur um Fotos – sondern um Erinnerungen"*
- *"nicht nur sehen... sondern wieder fühlen"*
- *"nicht nur in großen Gesten, sondern vor allem in den kleinen Momenten"*

**Why it works:** The rejection earns the claim. Every time she says "not only," the reader has been shown that she has already considered and moved past the obvious answer. By the time she offers the real thing, it lands with the authority of someone who has thought this through.

**Usage rule:** This construction must be used sparingly — only when both sides are genuinely earned. Overused, it becomes a tic. Used precisely, it is her most powerful rhetorical tool.

---

### 2.2 — She Grounds Every Abstraction in a Specific Detail

She never lets a general statement stand alone. Whenever she makes an abstract claim, she immediately collapses it into something sensory and particular.

**The pattern:**
*Abstract claim → colon or comma → specific sensory list*

- *"Augenblicke, die oft ganz nebenbei passieren: ein Blick, eine Berührung, das Gefühl eines Moments."*
- *"nicht nur fühlen, wie er sich angefühlt hat — die Aufregung am Morgen, die Tränen bei der Trauung, das Lachen mit Familie und Freunden"*
- *"Liebe zeigt sich... in den kleinen Momenten dazwischen"* → followed by: *"den stolzen Blick der Eltern, das Lächeln der Großeltern, die Freudentränen von Freunden"*

The specific detail is not decoration — it is the proof. She knows that abstract claims about emotion are unconvincing. The specific detail makes the emotion real.

**Usage rule:** Every abstract noun in copy must be followed within one or two sentences by at least one specific, sensory, physical detail.

---

### 2.3 — She Uses Precision Words at Moments of Conviction

Two words function as markers of her deepest conviction:

**Genau (exactly)**
She uses this to point at the precise thing that matters. It arrives at conclusions, not at openings. It says: *I have identified the specific thing here, and I am pointing directly at it.*

- *"Man kann sie nicht inszenieren und genau das macht sie so besonders."*
- *"Genau diese Details werden später oft zu den wertvollsten Erinnerungen."*
- *"Genau deshalb bedeutet Fotografie für mich so viel."*

**Vor allem (above all)**
She uses this to mark the most important item in a sequence — the thing that matters most when several things matter.

- *"und vor allem die Liebe, die sie an diesem Tag verbunden hat"*
- *"sondern vor allem in den kleinen Momenten dazwischen"*

**Usage rule:** Both words are load-bearing. *Genau* closes an argument; *vor allem* elevates the most important item in a list. Neither should be used as filler. Maximum once per paragraph.

---

### 2.4 — She Writes Toward the Future, Not the Present

Her sentences consistently move forward in time. The photographs are not the endpoint. The endpoint is years from now, when someone looks at the photograph and feels something.

- *"damit sie nicht verblassen und auch Jahre später wieder spürbar werden"*
- *"die euch auch Jahre später wieder genau in diesen einen Moment zurückversetzen"*
- *"Bilder bewahren Erinnerungen, Emotionen und Augenblicke, die niemals wieder genau so zurückkommen werden"*

**Usage rule:** Every piece of copy should contain awareness of the future moment — of looking back, of time passing, of the feeling that must survive. This is not a theme to be added selectively. It is her fundamental orientation.

---

### 2.5 — She Confesses Before She Claims

She establishes humanity and vulnerability before establishing ability. The most striking example from her interview: *"Ich war noch nie gut darin, Fotos zu erklären. Ich bin gut darin, hinzuschauen."*

The confession comes first — *I was never good at explaining photographs.* This is disarming. It is not what a brand would say. It is what a person says when they trust you enough to be honest. The claim that follows — *I am good at watching* — lands with far more authority because the preceding sentence proved she is not performing.

**Usage rule:** Where possible, Didem's voice should confess something true before claiming something true. The honesty of the confession makes the claim believable.

---

### 2.6 — She Does Not Explain What Can Be Felt

She never describes what an image shows, what a moment looks like, or what a wedding atmosphere is in visual terms. She describes what things *feel like* — the internal, not the external.

- *"die Aufregung am Morgen"* — not "the bride preparing in the morning light"
- *"die Tränen bei der Trauung"* — not "emotional ceremony moments"
- *"das Gefühl eines Moments"* — not "a beautiful captured instant"

**Usage rule:** Never describe appearances. Always describe feelings. The photograph handles the visual. The words handle the emotional interior.

## 3. REPEATED THEMES
### What She Returns To Without Prompting

These themes appear across both her founder description and her five interview answers without being requested. They are not strategic choices — they are her genuine preoccupations.

---

### Theme 01 — Kleine Momente (Small Moments)

**Frequency:** Appears in answers 1, 4, and 5. Three of five unprompted answers contain this phrase or the concept it carries.

**What she means:** Not the grand gestures, not the choreographed scenes, not the moments the wedding planner has scheduled. The moments that happen in the spaces between — the glance, the hand that reaches for another hand, the smile that crosses a grandmother's face when she watches her grandchild.

**Why it is central:** She has made a philosophical decision that the small moment is more valuable than the large one — specifically because it is unplanned, unposed, and therefore true. The large moments can be arranged. The small ones cannot.

---

### Theme 02 — Fühlen, nicht nur Sehen (Feeling, Not Only Seeing)

**Frequency:** Core of her positioning statement, present explicitly in answers 1, 2, and 5.

**What she means:** The distinction between the visual record of an event and the emotional experience of it. A photograph that shows what happened and a photograph that makes you feel what it was like to be there are two different objects. She makes the second kind.

**Why it is central:** This is the axis around which her entire practice turns. It is not a marketing position — it is the thing she actually believes.

---

### Theme 03 — Jahre später (Years Later)

**Frequency:** Appears explicitly in answers 1 and 2. Implicitly present in answer 5.

**What she means:** The primary audience for her photographs is not the couple as they exist today, making a purchasing decision. It is the couple as they will exist in ten or twenty years, sitting together and looking at something that takes them back. She makes photographs for that future person.

**Why it is central:** This temporal orientation is what separates her from photographers who think of their work as a product delivered at the end of a wedding day. She thinks of her work as something that will do its most important work years from now.

---

### Theme 04 — Nicht inszenieren (Cannot Be Staged)

**Frequency:** Stated explicitly in answer 3. Present implicitly throughout — her emphasis on *echte* (real) and *ehrlich* (honest) moments across all five answers.

**What she means:** The moments she values most are precisely the ones that cannot be arranged, posed, or directed. The first look is powerful *because* it is unrepeatable. The grandmother's smile happens *because* no one asked for it. Staging produces documents. The absence of staging produces truth.

**Why it is central:** This is a hard conviction, not a stylistic preference. It governs how she moves through the day, what she waits for, and what she believes makes a photograph worth preserving.

---

### Theme 05 — Bewusstseinslücke (The Consciousness Gap)

**Frequency:** Explicitly stated in answer 4. The concept underlies answers 1, 2, and 3.

**What she means:** Couples experience their wedding *wie im Flug* — in a blur. They are inside the day, overwhelmed, swept up. They cannot see clearly what is happening around them — the parents' pride, the friends' tears, the small gestures passing between themselves. They live the day but they cannot fully *observe* it. She is their eyes.

**Why it is central:** This theme gives her a specific and powerful role that goes beyond "capturing memories." She sees things the couple cannot see while living their day. She gives back moments they were too overwhelmed to notice. This is a gift, not a service.

---

### Theme 06 — Verblassen / Bewahren (Fading / Preserving)

**Frequency:** *Verblassen* (fade) in answer 1. *Bewahren* (preserve) in answer 5. The concept of protection against time runs throughout.

**What she means:** Memory is not stable. It fades. The feeling of a day — the specific texture of the excitement, the sound of the laughter, the weight of the emotion — these erode with time. Her photographs are the act of holding them before they go.

**Why it is central:** This is where her work intersects with something deeply human — the fear of forgetting. She does not talk about this fear explicitly, but her language consistently responds to it. *Damit sie nicht verblassen* — so that they do not fade. The entire practice is an answer to this fear.

## 5. WHAT SHE NEVER SOUNDS LIKE
### What Would Immediately Feel Fake or Commercial

### 5.1 — The Luxury Register

She does not speak in the language of luxury brands.

**Never use:**
*exklusiv (as aspiration) · premium · luxuriös · high-end · erstklassig · elite · auserlesen · maßgeschneidert*

The reason: these words position the work as a tier of service. Didem's work is not a tier — it is a conviction. Luxury vocabulary signals a vendor. Her voice signals a believer.

---

### 5.2 — The Romance-Cliché Register

Wedding photography has accumulated a set of phrases so overused they have become meaningless noise. Her voice contains none of them.

**Never use:**
*Märchen (fairytale) · traumhaft (dreamlike) · magisch (magical) · zauberhaft (enchanting) · unvergesslich (unforgettable) · Traumhochzeit (dream wedding) · der schönste Tag (the most beautiful day)*

The reason: these phrases have been used so many times they no longer carry feeling. Didem's entire practice is about the real over the romanticized. Using these words would directly contradict the philosophy that governs her work.

**Especially:** She never uses *unvergesslich* (unforgettable). She chooses instead to describe *specifically* what will be remembered and *exactly* how it will feel. The specific is always stronger than the superlative.

---

### 5.3 — The Perfection Register

She explicitly rejects perfection in favor of authenticity.

**Never use:**
*perfekt · makellos · ideal · der perfekte Moment · fehlerlos*

The reason: she stated directly that the moments she values most are the ones that cannot be staged. *"Man kann sie nicht inszenieren und genau das macht sie so besonders."* Perfection implies control. Her philosophy is about the beauty of what cannot be controlled.

---

### 5.4 — The Photography Industry Register

She does not describe her work in technical or industry terms.

**Never use:**
*Shooting · Session · Portfolio · Editing-Stil · Bildkomposition · Preset · Bokeh · golden hour · Belichtung · RAW-Dateien · Galerie (as a deliverable)*

The reason: technical vocabulary answers questions the visitor is not asking. They are not asking "how does she edit?" — they are asking "will she see our day the way it deserves to be seen?" Technical language also positions her as a service provider rather than an artist with a specific and irreplaceable way of seeing.

---

### 5.5 — The Corporate-Commercial Register

Her voice is personal, direct, and human. It is never formal, transactional, or institutional.

**Never use:**
*Dienstleistung (service) · Angebot (offer/package) · Buchung (booking) · Investition (investment) · Tarif (rate) · Leistungen (services/deliverables) · professionell (as a self-descriptor)*

The reason: these words belong to the vocabulary of a business relationship. Didem's voice establishes a human relationship — between a person who sees clearly and people who want their day seen that way. The commercial vocabulary breaks this register immediately.

---

### 5.6 — The Superlative Register

She makes no claims to being the best, the most, the only.

**Never use:**
*atemberaubend (breathtaking) · herzzerreißend (heartbreaking) · überwältigend (overwhelming) · außergewöhnlich (exceptional, as self-description)*

The reason: superlatives are the register of advertising, not testimony. Her voice testifies — it reports what she has seen and believed. Superlatives describe from the outside; testimony speaks from the inside.

---

### 5.7 — The Emotional-Excess Register

Her emotional language is precise, not amplified.

**Never use:**
Vague emotional nouns without specific context: *Emotionen* alone, *Gefühle* alone, *Liebe* alone (without a specific moment attached to it).

The reason: she never writes "emotion" — she writes "die Aufregung am Morgen, die Tränen bei der Trauung." Emotion without specificity is wallpaper. Emotion with a specific face and moment is truth.

## 6. SENTENCE RHYTHM
### How She Structures Thought

Didem's sentences have a measurable pulse. They are not random in length or structure. There are five distinct sentence patterns in her writing, and they alternate.

---

### Pattern A — The Plain Declaration

**Function:** States a belief or opens a position. Takes a side without hedging.
**Length:** Short. 8–14 words.
**Structure:** Subject + verb + claim. No qualifications. No "I think" or "I believe" as hedging prefixes — the conviction is in the directness, not in a stated belief marker.

*"Für mich sind Hochzeiten viel mehr als nur ein besonderer Tag."*
*"Liebe zeigt sich nicht nur in großen Gesten."*
*"Für mich geht es bei Hochzeiten nicht nur um Fotos."*

---

### Pattern B — The Expanding Specification

**Function:** Takes the declaration and gives it body through specific, sensory detail.
**Length:** Medium to long. 15–25 words.
**Structure:** The abstract concept expands into a list. Often introduced by a colon or the continuation of the previous sentence.

*"Es sind Tage voller echter Emotionen, bedeutender Begegnungen und kleiner Momente, die oft viel zu schnell vorbeigehen."*
*"Die Aufregung am Morgen, die Tränen bei der Trauung, das Lachen mit Familie und Freunden und vor allem die Liebe, die sie an diesem Tag verbunden hat."*

---

### Pattern C — The Pivot Construction

**Function:** Rejects the surface reading. Offers the deeper truth.
**Length:** Medium. 12–18 words.
**Structure:** *nicht nur [conventional reading] – sondern [the real thing]*. The dash is structural — it performs the pivot, separating what is being rejected from what is being offered.

*"Ich fotografiere Hochzeiten nicht nur so, wie sie aussehen – sondern so, wie sie sich anfühlen."*
*"nicht nur sehen, wie ihr Hochzeitstag aussah, sondern wieder fühlen, wie er sich angefühlt hat"*

---

### Pattern D — The Sensory List with Elevation

**Function:** Lists concrete sensory or emotional details, then elevates the most important with *vor allem*.
**Length:** Long. 20–30 words.
**Structure:** [item], [item], [item] und vor allem [the thing that matters most].

*"Die Aufregung am Morgen, die Tränen bei der Trauung, das Lachen mit Familie und Freunden und vor allem die Liebe, die sie an diesem Tag verbunden hat."*

The list builds. Each item is slightly more emotionally weighted than the last. *Vor allem* marks the peak. The reader arrives at the most important thing last, which means it stays longest.

---

### Pattern E — The Precise Close

**Function:** Closes a section of thought with a short, conclusive sentence. Often uses *genau* as the pointing word.
**Length:** Very short. 6–12 words.
**Structure:** Short declarative with *genau* pointing at the specific thing that has been established.

*"Man kann sie nicht inszenieren und genau das macht sie so besonders."*
*"Genau deshalb bedeutet Fotografie für mich so viel."*
*"Genau diese Details werden später oft zu den wertvollsten Erinnerungen."*

---

### The Alternating Rhythm

Her writing does not stay in one pattern. It moves through them:

```
A (declare) → B (expand) → C (pivot) → D (list with elevation) → E (close precisely)
```

Not always in this exact sequence — but the alternation between short and longer, between declaration and expansion, between abstract and specific, is consistent. The rhythm creates the sense of a mind that thinks clearly and speaks directly — never rambling, never too compressed.

**The dash (–):**
Used to perform a pivot, not merely to add information. It is stronger than a comma and more abrupt than a semicolon. In her writing, the dash announces: *what follows matters more than what preceded.*

**The colon (:):**
Used to open into specifics. It announces: *here is the exact thing I mean.* Always followed by a sensory list, never by a single abstraction.

## 7. AUDIENCE RELATIONSHIP
### How She Talks About Couples

---

### 7.1 — She Addresses Them Intimately

She uses *ihr* — the intimate German plural — not *Sie* (the formal). This is not a small decision. In German, the choice between *du/ihr* and *Sie* carries significant relational weight. She has chosen warmth and directness over professional distance. This must be maintained throughout all copy.

---

### 7.2 — She Addresses Their Future Selves

She does not address the couple as they currently exist — sitting at a laptop, deciding whether to book a photographer. She addresses the couple as they will be in ten or twenty years, looking at the photographs. The entire relational posture is oriented toward that future person.

*"die euch auch Jahre später wieder genau in diesen einen Moment zurückversetzen"*

**Implication for copy:** When Didem speaks to couples, she is speaking to their future selves. She is saying: *you will want this, later. You don't fully know it yet. I know it, because I have seen it.*

---

### 7.3 — She Demonstrates Understanding Before Offering a Solution

She never opens with what she does. She opens with what the couple experiences — specifically, the consciousness gap: they experience their day *wie im Flug*, in a blur, unable to fully see what is happening around them. She names this experience with precision. The couple recognizes themselves in it. Trust begins at the moment of recognition.

**The sequence:** *I know what your day is like → I see what you can't see → here is what that means*

---

### 7.4 — She Sees the Whole Emotional Ecosystem, Not Just the Couple

Her audience is not two people. It is two people and everyone who loves them. When she writes about couples, she includes the wider world of love that surrounds them:

*"den stolzen Blick der Eltern, das Lächeln der Großeltern, die Freudentränen von Freunden"*

Parents, grandparents, friends — she names them specifically. This has a specific effect on the couple reading: they recognize that she will see their day as a whole, not just as a portrait of two people. She will photograph love radiating outward, not only love focused inward.

---

### 7.5 — She Never Positions Herself Above Them

She does not write as an expert instructing a client. She writes as a person sharing something she believes, with people she already understands. The relational stance is one of mutual recognition — she understands their experience (the blur, the small moments they miss, the fear that it will all fade), and they will understand her work as the answer to that experience.

## 8. WEBSITE WRITING RULES
### The Standards Every Future Line of Copy Must Meet

---

**RULE 01 — First person. Always.**
Every line of website copy is written in Didem's voice, which means first person singular (*ich*). No third-person references to "Didem" in running copy. She is the voice, not the subject being described.

---

**RULE 02 — Every abstraction earns its place by being followed by a specific.**
If a sentence contains an abstract concept — *Emotionen, Erinnerungen, Liebe, Momente* — the next sentence or clause must contain a specific, sensory, physical detail. No abstract claim stands alone.

---

**RULE 03 — Feelings over appearances.**
Never describe what something looks like. Describe what it feels like. The visual is for the photographs. The words carry the emotional interior.

---

**RULE 04 — Confess before claiming.**
Where possible, acknowledge a limitation or a human truth before making a claim. The confession makes the claim believable. *"Ich war nie gut darin, Fotos zu erklären"* before *"Ich bin gut darin, hinzuschauen."*

---

**RULE 05 — Write toward the future, not the present.**
Every piece of copy should contain at least one orientation toward the future — the couple looking back, the memory that must survive, the feeling that will be accessible years from now. This is her fundamental temporal orientation. It must be present in every section.

---

**RULE 06 — Use the pivot construction with precision.**
*Nicht nur... sondern* is her most powerful rhetorical tool. It must be used sparingly — only when both the rejection and the offering are fully earned. Maximum once per section of copy.

---

**RULE 07 — *Genau* closes; *vor allem* elevates. Both carry weight.**
*Genau* should appear only at moments of genuine conviction, pointing at the specific thing that matters. *Vor allem* should appear only to mark the most important item in a sequence. Neither is filler.

---

**RULE 08 — Short sentences for beliefs. Longer sentences for specifics.**
The rhythm alternates. A short declaration is followed by an expanding sentence. An expanding sentence is followed by a short close. Monotony of length is monotony of voice.

---

**RULE 09 — *Perfekt* is forbidden.**
She believes in the real, not the perfect. The word *perfekt* directly contradicts her philosophy. Under no circumstances does this word appear in her copy.

---

**RULE 10 — Name the specific emotion, never "Emotion" alone.**
She writes *die Aufregung am Morgen, die Tränen bei der Trauung, das Lachen*. She does not write *die Emotionen des Tages*. Specific named feelings carry truth. Generalized emotional nouns carry nothing.

---

**RULE 11 — The luxury vocabulary is excluded.**
*Exklusiv, premium, luxuriös, high-end* — none of these words. They position the work as a tier of service. Her work is a conviction, not a tier.

---

**RULE 12 — The romance-cliché vocabulary is excluded.**
*Märchen, magisch, traumhaft, unvergesslich, Traumhochzeit* — none of these. They have been used so many times they no longer carry feeling. Her voice reaches for the specific, not the romantic shorthand.

---

**RULE 13 — No technical language.**
No photography jargon, no industry terms, no mention of equipment, editing, or technical process. The work is always described through its human purpose, never its technical production.

---

**RULE 14 — Address the couple as *ihr*, never *Sie*.**
The intimate plural throughout. Professional distance contradicts the entire brand. She is a person speaking to people, not a service provider addressing clients.

---

**RULE 15 — End on feeling, not action.**
Her sentences and sections close on the human thing, not the commercial thing. The last word she reaches for is always the emotional truth — *"die Liebe, die sie an diesem Tag verbunden hat"* — not a call to action. If a call to action is necessary, it follows the emotional close. It never replaces it.

---

**RULE 16 — Name the people in the room.**
She writes *Eltern, Großeltern, Freunde* — she names the specific people in the wider emotional world of a wedding. Generic language like *"loved ones"* or *"Ihre Lieben"* loses all the specific weight she gives to particular relationships.

---

**RULE 17 — No copy explains what an image shows.**
Text that describes what a visible image is showing is redundant and condescending. If an image shows two people touching foreheads, no copy should say "an intimate moment between the couple." The image already said it. The copy should say something the image cannot — the feeling, the significance, the time.

---

**RULE 18 — The sentence *"Ich melde mich persönlich bei euch"* is a model.**
Simple. Direct. Personal. The word *persönlich* carries the full weight of the brand promise — she is a person, not a system. Every line of copy should aspire to this quality: saying something large with very few words, with no attempt to impress.

## 9. VOICE VALIDATION TEST
### How to Test Whether a Sentence Sounds Like Didem

Every line of website copy must pass this test before it is approved for use.

---

### THE NINE QUESTIONS

**Question 01 — The Witness Test**
*Does this sentence sound like a person reporting something they have seen and believed — or like a brand describing its product?*
If brand: rewrite until it is witness.

**Question 02 — The Specificity Test**
*Does this sentence contain at least one specific, sensory, physical detail — or does it rest entirely on an abstract noun?*
If abstract only: add the specific detail or remove the sentence.

**Question 03 — The Feeling Test**
*Does this sentence describe how something feels — or how it looks?*
If visual only: rewrite to describe the interior experience.

**Question 04 — The Perfection Test**
*Does this sentence use any word from the forbidden vocabulary: perfekt, magisch, unvergesslich, traumhaft, Märchen, luxuriös, Shooting, premium?*
If yes: remove the word. Find the specific truth it was trying to approximate and say that instead.

**Question 05 — The Temporal Test**
*Does this sentence or its surrounding context contain awareness of time — of the future, of looking back, of memory surviving or fading?*
If the copy section contains no temporal dimension: add one.

**Question 06 — The Rhythm Test**
*Read it aloud. Does this sentence have a distinct length that contrasts with the sentence before and after it?*
If three sentences in a row are the same length: break the monotony. Shorten one. Expand another.

**Question 07 — The Intimacy Test**
*Does this sentence address the couple as *ihr* (intimate plural) or *Sie* (formal)?*
If formal: convert to intimate throughout.

**Question 08 — The Close Test**
*Does this sentence or section end on a feeling — or on a call to action?*
If it ends on a call to action: move the feeling before the action, or remove the action entirely if the section does not require one.

**Question 09 — The Confession Test**
*Is this sentence claiming something about Didem's ability or quality without any accompanying humanity or vulnerability?*
If it reads as pure credential: find the human truth underneath the claim and say that instead.

---

### THE FAST VALIDATION CHECKLIST

For quick copy review, apply this checklist to any sentence or paragraph:

```
□  Sounds like a person, not a brand
□  Contains a specific, sensory detail (not only abstract nouns)
□  Describes feeling, not appearance
□  Contains no forbidden vocabulary
□  Contains temporal awareness (somewhere in the surrounding copy)
□  Rhythm varies from surrounding sentences
□  Uses ihr, not Sie
□  Ends on the human truth, not the commercial ask
□  Does not explain what an image already shows
□  Could have come from her interview answers — not her marketing materials
```

**The final test — the single question:**

> *"Could Didem have said this in a conversation — or does it only exist because a copywriter wrote it?"*

If the honest answer is "only because a copywriter wrote it" — rewrite it until it sounds like something she would actually say to someone she had just met and trusted enough to speak honestly to.

That is her voice. Everything else is imitation.

## APPENDIX — HER VOICE IN ONE PARAGRAPH

If every element of this system were compressed into a single demonstration, it would read like this — not as website copy, but as a model of the voice in its natural state:

*Ich habe noch nie verstanden, warum man Hochzeiten fotografieren soll, damit sie schön aussehen. Sie sehen sowieso schön aus. Was verschwindet, wenn kein Bild da ist, ist das Gefühl. Die Aufregung am Morgen. Der Blick, den ein Paar wechselt, wenn es denkt, dass niemand hinschaut. Der stolze Blick der Eltern, während ihr Kind vor dem wichtigsten Menschen seines Lebens steht. Genau das ist es, wofür ich da bin — nicht um festzuhalten, wie es aussah. Sondern damit es spürbar bleibt.*

This paragraph was written to demonstrate the system, not for use on the website. It shows:
- First person, direct, no hedging
- Confession embedded in the opening
- Abstraction (Gefühl) immediately followed by specific sensory list
- The pivot construction (nicht... sondern)
- *Genau* as the precision close
- *Spürbar* — richer than *bewahren*
- Temporal orientation (bleibt — remains, endures)
- Feeling over appearance throughout
- No forbidden vocabulary
- Short and long sentences alternating
