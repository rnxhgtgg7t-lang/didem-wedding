## SECTION 01 — HERO

---

### Strategic Purpose

To end the visitor's comparison mode before it fully engages. Every visitor arrives scanning — efficient, slightly guarded, running the same evaluation they have run on other wedding photography websites. The hero's strategic job is not to win that evaluation. It is to make the evaluation irrelevant by operating in a register the visitor was not expecting. One image, one line, nothing more. The restraint itself is the first signal that something here is different.

---

### Emotional Purpose

To create a sensation in the body before the mind has time to process it. The visitor should feel something — a slowing, a pull, a quiet — before they can name what they are looking at. This is the only moment in the entire website where the emotional response can precede rational evaluation. Once the visitor begins reading, the rational mind re-engages. The hero must land before that happens.

---

### Key Message

*This is not what you were expecting.*

The key message is not stated — it is demonstrated. The image demonstrates it before any word is read.

---

### Supporting Messages

There are no supporting messages in the hero. The hero carries one message only. Every additional element dilutes the impact of that single message. The navigation is present but invisible. The wordmark is present but whispers. The positioning line is present but serves the image, not the other way around.

---

### Visitor Question Being Answered

*"Is this worth my time?"*

The answer is delivered before the question finishes forming. The image answers it at the level of sensation, not argument. The visitor does not decide to stay — they simply find themselves still there.

---

### What the Visitor Must Feel Before Leaving This Section

**Interrupted stillness.**

The scroll has slowed or stopped. The visitor has felt something they cannot immediately categorize. The image does not look like a wedding photograph — it looks like something from an editorial that belongs in a different context entirely. The visitor does not yet know why they have stopped. Only that they have.

---

### What Content Belongs Here

**The image.**
One photograph. The editorial tulle-gown hero. Full viewport width and height. No margins. The image does not sit behind content — it *is* the content. Everything else serves it or is invisible.

**The wordmark.**
DIDEM KANTEPER / WEDDING PHOTOGRAPHY — exactly as it appears in the logo. Small. Left-anchored. Present at the top, but not competing. The brand is named without being announced.

**The positioning line.**
One statement. Two lines maximum. The core of her philosophy, stated in her voice. This is the only text that carries direct meaning. It floats over the lower third of the image. It does not explain — it declares.

**The subline.**
One line only. Smaller than the positioning line. It extends the declaration without repeating it. It introduces a sensory or temporal dimension. It should feel like the second breath after the first.

**The scroll invitation.**
The smallest visible element on the page. A hairline arrow or a single word pointing down. Not a button. Not a call to action. Just the signal that there is more.

**The navigation.**
Present but invisible. Transparent over the hero. Logo left, four items right: STORIES · ABOUT · GALLERY · KONTAKT. Never competes with the image.

---

### What Content Does NOT Belong Here

| Content | Reason for exclusion |
|---|---|
| Multiple images or carousel | Destroys the singularity that creates impact |
| Pricing or package information | Returns visitor to evaluation mode immediately |
| Awards, badges, credentials | Positions Didem as a vendor competing for selection |
| Testimonials or client names | Introduces social proof logic — wrong register for the hero |
| "Book now" or "Get in touch" CTA | Arrives before trust is formed — premature and counterproductive |
| Number of weddings photographed | Credential language — belongs nowhere on this website |
| Social media follower counts | Quantification destroys the qualitative register |
| More than one line of subtext | Every additional line reduces the impact of the positioning line |
| Any element that can be compared to a competitor | Reactivates the comparison mode the hero is designed to end |

## SECTION 02 — BELIEF

---

### Strategic Purpose

To give the visitor the language for what they just experienced in the hero. The hero created a sensation without explanation. Left unnamed, that sensation creates discomfort — a feeling without a frame — which eventually prompts the visitor to leave. The Belief section resolves this by naming the distinction precisely: she photographs the way things felt, not just the way they looked. The visitor now has a philosophical frame through which to interpret everything they are about to see.

---

### Emotional Purpose

Recognition. The specific intellectual and emotional satisfaction of hearing something you already believed expressed precisely for the first time. The visitor should feel: *"Yes. That is exactly it. I have never heard it said that way. But that is exactly right."* This is the moment the first genuine thread of connection forms — not to the work, but to the mind behind the work.

---

### Key Message

*She does not photograph weddings the way they look. She photographs the way they feel.*

This message is not the visitor's first contact with the idea — the hero image has already demonstrated it. The Belief section names what the hero showed. It is confirmation, not introduction.

---

### Supporting Messages

**This distinction is philosophical, not stylistic.**
Other photographers choose a style. She has chosen a position. The difference is significant — a style can be replicated; a position cannot. The supporting message should carry the weight of conviction, not the lightness of preference.

**The feeling is what survives time. The appearance is what fades.**
This temporal dimension, if present in the supporting text, connects the Belief section to the deeper brand truth — that she preserves feelings before time changes them. It should arrive briefly, not as a separate argument, but as a natural continuation of the key message.

---

### Visitor Question Being Answered

*"What am I looking at? Why does this feel so different from everything else I've seen?"*

The Belief section does not answer this with a list of differentiators. It answers it by naming the fundamental principle that governs every image the visitor will see from this point forward.

---

### What the Visitor Must Feel Before Leaving This Section

**Recognition and alignment.**

The visitor should feel they have just heard something true — something they agreed with before they knew they agreed with it. They are no longer simply looking at a wedding photography website. They are reading the work of someone who thinks differently about what photography is for. The evaluation mode has not returned. Something warmer has taken its place: the beginning of belief.

---

### What Content Belongs Here

**The core positioning statement.**
Her primary philosophical statement, in her voice. Two lines maximum. Set large enough to carry full attention. Centered on the page. No competition from any other element.

**A hairline rule.**
A thin horizontal line beneath the positioning statement. Not decorative — structural. It marks the pivot between the main statement and the supporting line. A visual breath.

**One supporting line.**
A single sentence that extends the positioning statement without repeating it. It should introduce either the temporal dimension (what survives time) or the witnessing dimension (what she sees that others miss). Maximum one sentence. In her voice — direct, warm, without flourish.

**White space.**
Not as a design choice. As a structural requirement. The Belief section must be given more vertical breathing room than any other text-dominant section on the page. The white space allows the statement to land fully before the visitor moves forward.

---

### What Content Does NOT Belong Here

| Content | Reason for exclusion |
|---|---|
| Multiple paragraphs | Breaks the concentration that makes the statement powerful |
| Any image | The deliberate absence of image after the image-dominant hero creates maximum emphasis on the words |
| Service or practical information | Immediately returns visitor to evaluation mode at the wrong moment |
| Call to action | Premature — trust has not been established; a CTA here would feel like a sales pitch |
| Social proof or testimonials | The Belief section is philosophy — introducing evidence at this moment undermines the philosophical register |
| Any list of features or differentiators | Reduces a conviction to a feature list |
| Anything requiring more than 20 seconds to read | The Belief section is a pause, not a reading session |

## SECTION 03 — LEONORA & DENIS

---

### Strategic Purpose

To transform philosophy into demonstrated experience. The visitor now understands what Didem believes — they need to see that this belief produces something genuinely real and emotionally true when applied to actual people on an actual day. Beyond proof, this section transfers desire: the visitor watches someone else's story and begins, quietly, to imagine their own. It is the first moment where the visitor moves from observer to aspirant.

---

### Emotional Purpose

Longing that becomes personal. The visitor watches Leonora and Denis's story and somewhere inside the watching begins to project — begins to feel what it would be like to have their own love seen and preserved this carefully. The shift from "that is beautiful" to "I want that for us" happens in this section. It does not announce itself. It happens through accumulated feeling.

---

### Key Message

*The philosophy produces something real, human, and deeply felt — not just claimed.*

The key message is delivered by the images, not by any text. The text's job is to give the story a name and a voice — to frame it as a story, not a portfolio entry.

---

### Supporting Messages

**Every story is told individually.**
Leonora and Denis's story belongs to them. It is not a template applied to a couple — it is an account of what was true on their specific day. This specificity is itself a message: the visitor's story will receive the same individual attention.

**The small moments hold the most weight.**
If the image selection is done correctly, this message is carried by the photographs chosen — not by a moment of ceremony or posed portrait, but by the look, the touch, the gesture that happens between the large events. The text can acknowledge this, briefly.

**Location and year are context, not decoration.**
Knowing where and when the story took place grounds it in reality. It answers the implicit question: "Is this real? Did this actually happen?" Yes — here, and then.

---

### Visitor Question Being Answered

*"Does this philosophy actually produce something? Can she hold a real wedding on a real day and make it feel this way?"*

The answer is the story itself. Text has a supporting role only.

---

### What the Visitor Must Feel Before Leaving This Section

**Moved and aspirational.**

Not the abstract admiration of a viewer watching someone else's beautiful photographs. The personal ache of wanting something for themselves. The visitor should leave this section with the beginning of desire — not fully formed, not yet certain, but present. The internal thought: *"I want our story told this way."*

---

### What Content Belongs Here

**The opening image.**
One large photograph — not full-bleed, but near-full, with slight margins. The most emotionally powerful single frame from the Leonora and Denis story. Not the most technically perfect — the most emotionally true. This image does not need to show both people — it needs to show a moment.

**The story title.**
Their names, large, editorial in scale. This is a title, not a label. LEONORA & DENIS. It names the story the way a chapter is named — with dignity and specificity.

**Location and year.**
Small, secondary, right-aligned beside the couple's names. One line. City or region and year only — not the full venue name, not logistical detail.

**The editorial image spread.**
Three images arranged asymmetrically — one tall portrait spanning the full height of the zone in the left column; two images stacked in the right column (one taller portrait above, one square or landscape below). Never equal-sized. Never a grid. The asymmetry is intentional — it creates editorial rhythm and signals that these were placed by a person with a point of view.

**Two to three lines of story text.**
In Didem's voice. Narrative, not descriptive. The text should name the feeling of the day — not describe what the images show. It exists to give the story a voice, not to caption the photographs. This is the most minimal possible written presence that still gives the story a human frame.

**The story invitation.**
A text link — not a button — aligned to the right. Small. Directional. An invitation to see the full story, not a command to act.

---

### What Content Does NOT Belong Here

| Content | Reason for exclusion |
|---|---|
| Captions describing what images show | The image describes itself; text that describes it is redundant and condescending |
| Technical details (equipment, settings, time of day) | Moves from human story to technical document — wrong register entirely |
| Venue promotional language | This is Leonora and Denis's story, not the venue's advertisement |
| Multiple paragraphs of story text | Text competes with images; the images must dominate |
| Image count or delivery specifications | Belongs to a pricing conversation, not a story |
| Any copy that could apply to any wedding | Generic language destroys the individuality that is the point |
| Before/after or comparison imagery | The work needs no justification; comparison implies doubt |

## SECTION 04 — AYCAN & ERGIN

---

### Strategic Purpose

To dissolve the last rational excuse for not trusting Didem completely. After one story, the rational mind makes an escape: perhaps it was the venue, the couple, a lucky day. The second story removes that excuse by delivering the same interior quality across a completely different aesthetic register, emotional register, and visual language. It converts admiration — which can always be attributed to circumstance — into conviction, which cannot.

---

### Emotional Purpose

Trust arrived at through a second act of longing. The visitor is moved again, differently, and in being moved twice — differently — concludes that this is not luck. This is her. The rational escape route closes. Something that has been forming since the first story solidifies: an internal decision that has not yet been expressed.

---

### Key Message

*This quality is consistent. Different day, different couple, different light — the same depth of seeing.*

Again, the key message is delivered by the images. The structural differences from Story 01 — the B&W register, the title-first opening, the full-bleed punctuation image — carry the message that this is a different chapter while the interior quality confirms it is the same author.

---

### Supporting Messages

**She sees across registers.**
The deliberate use of B&W as the dominant visual language of this story communicates that her eye is not dependent on colour, light, or setting. She sees in any register.

**The emotional peak stops the scroll.**
The full-bleed punctuation image — the most powerful frame of this story — must land as the most emotionally charged image on the homepage outside of the hero. It exists to stop the visitor's movement entirely. To make them hold. This image carries no supporting message. It is the message.

**Location and year anchor this in reality.**
As with Story 01 — the specificity of place and time confirms that this is real.

---

### Visitor Question Being Answered

*"Is she always like this? Or was the first story exceptional circumstances?"*

The second story's existence is the answer. Its difference from the first story is the proof.

---

### What the Visitor Must Feel Before Leaving This Section

**Conviction.**

Not the aspirational longing of Story 01 — something quieter and more resolute. The rational escape route has been closed. The visitor can no longer explain away what they are seeing. The internal decision forms: *"This is her. Not the circumstances — her. She can hold any kind of day. She would hold ours."*

---

### What Content Belongs Here

**The title-first opening.**
Unlike Story 01, which opens with an image, this story opens with the couple's names. Large, editorial, left-aligned. This deliberate reversal signals immediately: different chapter, different energy. The name arrives before any image — an inversion that the visitor registers even without analysing it.

**Location and year.**
Same treatment as Story 01 — small, secondary, positioned near the couple's names.

**The editorial image spread.**
Three images, arranged asymmetrically but mirrored from Story 01's column structure. One very tall vertical image in the left column (the most sustained single presence in the spread); one wide cinematic crop and one square intimate detail stacked in the right column. All three in B&W. The arrangement communicates: same skill, different grammar.

**The full-bleed punctuation image.**
100% viewport width. The most emotionally powerful frame of the Aycan & Ergin story — the first dance, the bouquet, the moment of pure feeling. This image is the emotional peak of the entire homepage. It is the frame the visitor will hold longest. It must be chosen with absolute editorial intention.

**Two to three lines of story text.**
In Didem's voice. Left-aligned (not centred as in Story 01 — the alignment difference continues the differentiation). Narrative, not descriptive. Names the feeling of the day without explaining the images.

**The story invitation.**
Same treatment as Story 01 — text link, right-aligned, small, directional.

---

### What Content Does NOT Belong Here

| Content | Reason for exclusion |
|---|---|
| Anything that feels like a repeat of Story 01 | Sameness reads as a single trick; difference reads as genuine range |
| Explanation of the B&W aesthetic choice | The choice speaks for itself; explaining it reduces it |
| Captions or descriptive copy | Same reason as Story 01 |
| Any element that softens the emotional impact of the punctuation image | The punctuation image must land at full force |
| Copy that references Story 01 | Each story stands alone; cross-referencing breaks the individual dignity of each |

## SECTION 05 — ABOUT DIDEM

---

### Strategic Purpose

To complete the trust equation. Desire (from Story 01), conviction (from Story 02), and now the final requirement: trust in the person, not just the work. The visitor needs to know who will be in the room with them on the most emotionally concentrated day of their life. No portfolio can answer this question. Only her own voice, speaking as herself — warm, direct, without performance — can.

---

### Emotional Purpose

Connection. The warm, specific feeling of recognising a person rather than recognising a service. The visitor should feel they are meeting someone — briefly, through a screen — whose way of seeing the world aligns with their own. Not "she seems good at her job." Something warmer: *"She would understand us. I would want her there."*

---

### Key Message

*She sees what others miss — and she will see us, too.*

This message is carried by the combination of her portrait (a person who sees) and her words (evidence that she has seen truly, and understood why it matters).

---

### Supporting Messages

**She works quietly.**
Her presence is calm, not intrusive. She moves through the day as an observer, not a director. Couples almost forget she is there — and that is when the truest images happen. This message addresses the specific anxiety: "Will she disrupt our day?"

**She sees what the couple cannot see while living the day.**
The consciousness gap — couples experience their wedding in a blur; she sees the parents' pride, the grandparents' smile, the small gestures between the couple themselves that the couple will never consciously have seen. She gives them back their day more completely than they lived it.

**She believes the small moment holds more truth than the large one.**
Not the choreographed entrance, not the posed formal portrait — the look that passes between two people in an unguarded moment. This is what she waits for. This is what she preserves.

**She understands that the day passes too quickly.**
She has seen it enough times to know. The day goes *wie im Flug* — in a blur. This is why she is there. Not to document it from the outside, but to hold the pieces of it that would otherwise dissolve before the week is out.

---

### Visitor Question Being Answered

*"Who is she? Would I feel comfortable with her on our day? Would she understand us?"*

The question is not "is she good?" — the stories already answered that. The question is personal and specific: could I be myself around her? Would she see us?

---

### What the Visitor Must Feel Before Leaving This Section

**Safe and connected.**

The specific relief of knowing that the person who will be in the room is someone they trust — not because of credentials, but because they have recognised her. *"She thinks the way I want to think about my own wedding. She would see what I'd want her to see. And she would understand if I'm not perfectly composed."*

---

### What Content Belongs Here

**Her opening personal line.**
The first thing her voice says in this section — before her portrait appears. Her words arrive before her face. This reversal is intentional: voice before image signals that who she is comes before how she looks. One sentence or two. Personal. In her register: direct, warm, without performance. The model is her interview opener — honest about what she is not before she is confident about what she is.

**Her portrait.**
One photograph. Large, editorial, contained (not full-bleed). A 2:3 portrait crop. This is not a headshot — it is a photograph of a person, made with the same eye that made every other image on the page. She should appear as she is: composed, present, the quality of attention visible in her expression. This image should answer "what does she look like?" in a way that also answers "what is she like?"

**Her personal statement — first paragraph.**
Who she is and how she works. Written in first person, in German, in her voice. This paragraph should address her method: quiet presence, waiting for the real moment, moving through the day without directing it. It should contain at least one specific, sensory detail — not a general description of how she works, but a particular thing she notices or waits for.

**Her personal statement — second paragraph.**
What she believes about weddings and memory. This paragraph should carry the temporal dimension — the consciousness gap, the day that passes too quickly, what photographs can give back. It should name the specific things she sees: the parents' pride, the grandparents' smile, the small gestures. It should feel like testimony, not manifesto.

**Her personal statement — third paragraph (optional).**
The deepest conviction: why photography matters to her. This is where her signature belief belongs — that photographs preserve what time would otherwise take. Brief. The emotional close of the statement.

**Her signature line.**
Set apart, visually distinct, larger than body text. Her core statement in her own words. The one line that contains everything. It functions as both a pull-quote and a closing mark — the last thing the visitor holds from this section before moving to the gallery.

---

### What Content Does NOT Belong Here

| Content | Reason for exclusion |
|---|---|
| Credential lists (awards, publications, recognitions) | Answers "is she good?" which the stories already answered; wrong question for this section |
| Number of weddings photographed | Quantification destroys the qualitative register |
| Pricing or service information | Reactivates evaluation mode at the wrong moment |
| Technical information about her process or equipment | Irrelevant to the question this section is answering |
| Copy written in the third person ("Didem is...") | She speaks in first person; third person introduces brand-distance she does not have |
| Testimonials in this section | The section works by Didem's own voice earning trust; external validation would undermine the directness of the approach |
| Claims to being "the best" or comparative positioning | Her voice never positions against others; she positions through conviction, not comparison |
| Multiple photographs | One portrait only; more images divide the attention that should be concentrated on her as a person |
| Any copy that reads as performance | The voice validation test must be applied rigorously here — every line must sound like something she would actually say |

## SECTION 06 — GALLERY

---

### Strategic Purpose

To dissolve the final rational objections through visual accumulation. The visitor is emotionally decided — they want Didem for their wedding. But the rational mind makes one last stand with specific, practical questions: What if our venue is different? What if we're not photogenic? What if those two stories were her best work? The gallery answers all of these simultaneously, without a single word, by showing range, consistency, and command across different kinds of couples, light, moments, and aesthetics.

---

### Emotional Purpose

Calm certainty. Not the aspirational longing of the stories or the warm recognition of the About section — something quieter. The specific quality of a decision that has settled into the body and become real. The visitor should leave the gallery with nothing left to question.

---

### Key Message

*There is no version of our day she could not hold.*

This message is carried entirely by the images. No text is needed. No text belongs.

---

### Supporting Messages

There are no supporting messages stated in text. The images carry all messages:
- Range: different kinds of moments, different aesthetics, different emotional registers
- Consistency: the same interior quality of seeing across all of them
- Command: full-bleed images that carry the weight of genuine conviction

---

### Visitor Question Being Answered

*"Is she this good consistently? Can she hold different kinds of couples, light, venues, and days? Would she be able to see our day the same way?"*

The gallery answers these questions through accumulation, not argument.

---

### What the Visitor Must Feel Before Leaving This Section

**Certainty, quiet and complete.**

The decision that has been forming since the hero is now settled. The visitor is no longer seeking evidence — they have enough and more. What remains is only the question of how to begin. *"I don't need to see more. I already know."*

---

### What Content Belongs Here

**The opening full-bleed image.**
100% viewport width. 65–70% viewport height. The most emotionally powerful single frame in the gallery — chosen for emotional impact, not technical perfection. This image must justify a full stop in the visitor's scroll. It announces the gallery not with a title but with a statement.

**The paired editorial zones.**
Two zones, each containing three images in an asymmetric two-column arrangement. Images of different proportions — one tall portrait, one landscape, one square — never the same crop ratio adjacent to each other. The visual rhythm is intentional, not algorithmic.

**The solo centred image.**
One image, 70–72% viewport width, centred, surrounded by white space. This image breathes alone. Its purpose is to give the visitor a moment of stillness within the sequence — and to demonstrate that a single image, given space, carries complete weight.

**The closing full-bleed image.**
100% viewport width. Slightly smaller than the opener — 60–65% viewport height. Quieter in emotional register. Intimate rather than dramatic. The closing image does not compete with the opener — it settles after it. It is the last image the visitor sees before the inquiry invitation arrives.

**B&W and colour, deliberately alternated.**
Never two B&W images adjacent. Never two high-colour images adjacent. The alternation creates visual rhythm and ensures that no image's aesthetic register reads as an accident.

**Eight to twelve images total on the homepage.**
Enough to prove consistency and range. Not so many that the curation becomes invisible. Luxury is restraint. Ten strong images say more than thirty adequate ones.

---

### What Content Does NOT Belong Here

| Content | Reason for exclusion |
|---|---|
| Any text (captions, labels, titles) | The gallery's power is the deliberate absence of text — adding any breaks the immersive sequence |
| A section header ("GALLERY" or equivalent) | Announcing the gallery as a feature of a website rather than an experience destroys the editorial register |
| Navigation arrows or browsing controls | Turns an immersive sequence into a browsable portfolio — the wrong mode entirely |
| Images weaker than the standard set by the featured stories | Every frame must justify its presence; one weak image lowers the perceived quality of all adjacent images |
| Equal-sized thumbnail grids | Grid logic is algorithmic; curation logic is editorial; only the second belongs here |
| Technical metadata (camera settings, location tags) | Wrong register; wrong question |
| More than twelve images | More images do not mean more confidence; they mean less curation — and less curation reads as doubt |

## SECTION 07 — INQUIRY

---

### Strategic Purpose

To give the visitor somewhere to place a decision already made internally. The inquiry is not the moment of decision — that moment happened somewhere between the About section and the end of the gallery. The inquiry's strategic job is to make the act of reaching out feel like the natural continuation of a conversation that has been happening since the first image, not a gear-change into commercial transaction.

---

### Emotional Purpose

Ease and anticipation. The specific relief of acting on something that has been building since the hero. And beneath it — the quiet anticipation of a first real conversation with someone the visitor already trusts. The inquiry should feel like replying to someone who has already been speaking to you, warmly, for the length of an afternoon.

---

### Key Message

*Tell me about your day.*

Not "get in touch." Not "book now." Not "request a quote." The key message is an invitation into a conversation — warm, specific, personal. It presupposes that the visitor has a story worth telling, and that she wants to hear it.

---

### Supporting Messages

**She wants to know how the day should feel, not just the logistics.**
The form and its language should signal that she is interested in the emotional character of their day before she is interested in the date and the venue. The open textarea — "Erzählt mir von eurem Tag..." — is the primary field, not a secondary one.

**She responds personally.**
This is not an automated system. A person — specifically, Didem — will read this and reply. The closing line names this directly. It is a promise that carries the brand truth: she is a person, not a process.

**The conversation has already begun.**
The entire website has been a form of address — she has been speaking to the visitor throughout. The inquiry is the moment the visitor replies. The copy should carry this sense: not a new beginning, but a continuation.

---

### Visitor Question Being Answered

*"How do I begin?"*

Not "should I?" — the gallery answered that. Not "can I afford it?" — this section is not the place for that conversation. Simply: how do I begin the conversation? The inquiry answers it with the smallest possible friction.

---

### What the Visitor Must Feel Before Leaving This Section

**That they have opened a door, not submitted a form.**

The act of sending the inquiry should feel like the natural exhale at the end of a journey already completed. Not a risk taken — a decision expressed. Not a transaction initiated — a relationship begun. The visitor should close the tab or move on feeling: *"I'm glad I did that. This is already different."*

---

### What Content Belongs Here

**The heading.**
Warm, personal, in her voice. Not "Kontakt" or "Anfrage." An invitation — the same way she would begin a conversation if the visitor walked into her studio. The heading should feel like the first sentence of a letter she wrote specifically for them.

**Two to three lines of invitation copy.**
In her voice. These lines should do two things: explain what she is asking for (tell me about your day), and signal how she will receive it (personally, with genuine interest in how it should feel, not just the logistics). Maximum three lines. Every word earns its place.

**The form — four single-line fields.**
- Their names (plural — this is addressed to both of them)
- Date of the wedding
- Location or venue
- How they found Didem (optional — useful for her, low-friction for them)

All four fields use hairline bottom borders only — no visible box, no background fill. Labels float above on focus. The form should feel like writing lines on elegant paper, not filling in a database entry.

**The form — one open textarea.**
*"Erzählt mir von eurem Tag..."* — her language, her tone. This is the most important field. It invites them to share feeling, not facts. No word limit. No character count. The prompt is an open question, not a required field.

**The send action.**
Text with a directional arrow — not a filled button. "Nachricht senden →" — understated, calm. The visual weight of the send action should be no greater than the weight of the field labels.

**The personal closing line.**
Below the form. Outside its container. Centered. The smallest text on the page. Her promise: she will respond personally. This line carries the most emotional weight per character of anything on the page. It is the last thing the visitor reads. It must be in her voice and it must be true.

---

### What Content Does NOT Belong Here

| Content | Reason for exclusion |
|---|---|
| Pricing information or package names | Reactivates evaluation mode at the precise moment it needs to remain closed; pricing is a conversation, not a precondition |
| Urgency language ("limited availability," "book before it's gone") | Contradicts the warmth and ease the section is designed to create; she does not pressure |
| Multiple CTAs or alternative actions | Introduces decision fatigue at the moment of maximum commitment |
| FAQ content | Signals that the visitor needs more information before proceeding — the journey should have made this unnecessary |
| Testimonials or social proof | The section is personal and private in register; third-party voices break the intimacy |
| Social media links or handles | Sends the visitor away at the moment of highest engagement |
| A map or address | Implies a transactional, in-person-office register — wrong for this relationship |
| Any language that positions the inquiry as a business request | Every word must preserve the sense that this is a conversation being opened, not a service being requested |
| More than five form fields | Each additional field increases friction and reduces the sense of personal invitation |

## THE CONTENT FLOW LAWS

*These govern how content must move from section to section across the entire page.*

---

**LAW 01 — Each section answers only one question.**
When a section tries to answer more than one question, it dilutes both answers. The single question each section exists to answer is defined above. No section acquires additional jobs without strategic review.

**LAW 02 — Text volume decreases where image volume increases.**
The hero is maximum image, minimum text. The gallery is maximum image, zero text. The About section is maximum text, minimum image. The inquiry is maximum text, zero image. These are not aesthetic preferences — they are the content logic of the emotional journey.

**LAW 03 — No section reaches into the next section's territory.**
The Belief section does not offer proof — that is the Stories' job. The Stories do not introduce Didem personally — that is the About's job. The About section does not show range and consistency — that is the Gallery's job. Each section completes its own job and stops.

**LAW 04 — White space between sections is structural, not decorative.**
The gap between every section is a minimum of 120px on desktop, 100px on mobile. This space is not empty — it is where the visitor processes what they have just experienced before receiving what comes next. Removing white space compresses the emotional processing time and reduces the impact of every section.

**LAW 05 — The call to action appears once, at the end.**
There is no "Book now" button in the hero. No "Get in touch" in the About section. No inquiry form link in the middle of the gallery. The entire website builds toward one moment of invitation. Distributing calls to action throughout the page signals desperation and returns the visitor to evaluation mode prematurely.

**LAW 06 — No section explains what the visitor can already feel.**
If the images show something emotional, the text does not describe that emotion. If the story is moving, the story text does not say "this is a moving story." The visitor experiences directly — text that narrates or explains the experience condescends to the experience and to the visitor.

**LAW 07 — The inquiry arrives when the visitor is ready, not before.**
The sequence exists for one reason: to move the visitor from skeptical browser to certain believer before the inquiry is offered. If any section is removed or reordered, the visitor arrives at the inquiry before they are ready, and the inquiry becomes a request from a stranger rather than a continuation of a conversation with someone already trusted.

### What Every Copywriter Needs Before Writing a Single Word

**The voice reference:** DIDEM-VOICE-SYSTEM.md — open it. Every line written must pass the Voice Validation Test at the end of that document.

**The narrative reference:** The narrative architecture established in the strategy — each section exists in a specific psychological sequence. Copy that does not serve that sequence should not exist.

**The three questions for every line:**

1. *Does this sound like Didem — or like a brand writing copy in her name?*
2. *Does this advance the visitor's emotional journey — or pause it?*
3. *Does this belong in this section — or is it doing another section's job?*

> *The photographs carry the visual. The copy carries the interior.*
> Text describes feeling, not appearance.
> Images describe the day. Text describes what it was like to be inside it.
